## Eleven models (week): skeleton = feasible set + preferences + tangency/best response + welfare verdict (efficiency & fairness)
1 Malthusian (W1) TE §2.7–2.8 model, §2.9–2.10 data; Fig 2.16. Concepts: diminishing average product of labour, subsistence level, real wage, technology, Malthusian trap. Two panels: real wage vs population (downward, fixed land → diminishing AP); population growth vs real wage (upward; zero at subsistence). Equilibrium A: wage = subsistence, pop growth 0. Technology ↑ shifts wage curve up → wage above subsistence → population grows → back to subsistence with bigger population. Plague (pop fall) → B higher wage → pop recovers → back to A. Regression → smaller population. Fits pre-1700; fails after Industrial Revolution (sustained tech progress outran pop; demographic transition). Misconception: equilibrium is subsistence, not starvation.
2 Utility maximisation (W2,6) TE Unit 3: §3.2 ICs & MRS, §3.3 opportunity cost, §3.4 feasible set/frontier/budget & MRT, §3.7 income & substitution effects; Varian Ch 2–5 except §5.6 (§3.3, §3.4, §3.6–3.8, §4.3); Figs 3.10 (grade vs free time; optimum E at 19 hours free time, grade 57; B: MRT>MRS; A: MRS>MRT), 3.15 (consumption vs free time), Varian Ch5. ICs can't cross (transitivity). Well-behaved = monotonic + convex (diminishing MRS). MRS=MU1/MU2 slope of IC; MRT slope of frontier. Free time on x-axis (good, not hours worked = bad). Budget c=w(T−t) slope −w; p1x1+p2x2=m slope −p1/p2, intercepts m/p2, m/p1. Wage rise: substitution (less free time) vs income (more free time if normal) → ambiguous. Lottery win: income effect only. Preference types: Cobb–Douglas x1^a x2^b → x1=a m/((a+b)p1) shares; perfect substitutes ax1+bx2 → corner compare a/p1 vs b/p2; perfect complements min{ax1,bx2} → ax1=bx2; quasilinear v(x1)+x2 → v'(x1)=p1/p2 no income effect on good 1. Monotonic transformations change nothing (√(x1 x2^4) same as x1x2^4: share on good 2 = 4/5). Diminishing MRS ≠ diminishing MU (E³B²).
3 Profit maximisation, price-setting firm (W3,5) TE Unit 7: §7.4 demand & isoprofits, §7.7 CS/PS, Pareto efficiency, DWL, §7.8 elasticity; Varian Ch24 §24.4–24.5; price discrimination (Week 4 pre-recorded lecture; Varian Ch25 "more details than you need"); Fig 7.11 + MR. Isoprofits = firm's ICs; demand = feasible frontier; optimum tangency ⇔ MR=MC. AC = zero economic profit curve (isoprofit π=0). MC = price-taker's supply curve. ε=(dQ/dP)(P/Q); linear demand elastic top, unit elastic midpoint, inelastic bottom. MR=P(1+1/ε) >0 iff |ε|>1 → monopolist on elastic part. Markup (P−MC)/P=1/|ε|. Not Pareto efficient (P>MC) → DWL. First-degree price discrimination: CS→0, DWL→0, efficient but unequal. Linear P=a−bQ → MR=a−2bQ.
4 Market S&D, price-taking firms (W4,5) TE Unit 8: §8.3 price-taking, §8.6 exogenous shocks, §8.7 taxation, §8.8–8.9 competitive equilibrium; §11.1, §11.3 SR/LR; Fig 11.6 (bakery firm & bread market). Characteristics: many buyers/sellers, homogeneous product, free entry/exit, good information. P=MC. Exogenous vs endogenous shocks (cheaper flour → supply right). SR: number of firms fixed, S=D; LR: entry/exit → P=min AC, zero profit. Profit per unit = P−AC. Per-unit tax t shifts supply up by t; Pc, Ps=Pc−t; revenue tQ; DWL triangle; legal incidence irrelevant; proportional τ pivots supply (steeper by 1/(1−τ)). Incidence on more inelastic side. Firm supply curve = MC above min AC (shut down below) [guide's statement].
5 Price dynamics & environmental dynamics (W4) TE §11.8 (Fig 11.17), §20.8 (Fig 20.24 sea ice). Positive/negative feedback, stable/unstable equilibria, tipping point. 45° line diagram X_{t+1} vs X_t; equilibrium = crossing; stable if slope<1 (flatter), unstable if >1. Cobweb/staircase tracing P1→P2→P3→P0. Sea ice S-curve: C low stable, A middle unstable (tipping point), B high stable; climate change shifts curve down: 3 → 2 (A,B merge tangency) → 1 equilibrium (only C). Marble-in-bowl analogy.
6 Production & cost minimisation (W5) Varian §20.1; Week 6 lecture slides diagram. RTS: F(tK,tL)=t^k F: k>1 IRS, =1 CRS, <1 DRS; economies/diseconomies of scale; MPL, MPK; isocost c=wL+rK slope −w/r; isoquant slope −MPL/MPK (TRS). Tangency MPL/MPK=w/r ⇔ MPL/w=MPK/r. Diagram points A (optimum), B, C on higher isocost, D below isoquant (cheaper but infeasible). FLAG: concave isoquant (K²+L²)^{1/2} → tangency is maximum cost; corners. Perfect substitutes compare r/a vs w/b; perfect complements aK=bL=y. RTS ≠ marginal product; IRS ≠ economies of scale exactly.
7 Games & social preferences (W8) TE Unit 4: §4.1 game, payoff matrix; §4.2 best response, dominant strategy (equilibrium); §4.6–4.7 public goods game; §4.9 social preferences altruism, inequality aversion, reciprocity; §4.10 ultimatum game; §4.11 experimental evidence (Kenyan farmers, US students); §4.13 Nash equilibrium, climate change game. Figs 4.2b (Rice–Cassava: Anil rows; (Rice,Rice)=(1,3), (Rice,Cassava)=(2,2), (Cassava,Rice)=(4,4), (Cassava,Cassava)=(3,1); Anil dominant Cassava, Bala dominant Rice; DSE (Cassava,Rice)=(4,4)), 4.11 (ultimatum $100). Other games: cow herders (tragedy of commons), public goods, climate change, keeping up with the Joneses, window tax, number guessing. NE: no one gains by deviating unilaterally; 0,1 or many; DSE ⇒ NE; NE ⇏ Pareto efficient (social dilemma). Ultimatum selfish: accept any x>0, offer minimal; real offers ≈ half, low rejected. Utility signatures: altruism u=xi+αxj; inequality aversion u=xi−β|xi−xj|; reciprocity; spite (additional). Reciprocity rejecting y gives R(40−y): accept iff y≥40R/(1+R) (R=1:20; R=4:32). Anil sharing £100: U=xB → give all; U=xA^0.3 xB^0.7 → 30/70; U=xA+xB indifferent; U=min{2xA,xB} → 33.33/66.67.
8 Labour discipline (W9) TE Unit 6: §6.5 employment rent, reservation wage; §6.6 best response function; §6.7 isocosts for effort, involuntary unemployment, efficiency wage; Figs 6.3 (employment rent: wage $12, benefit $6, +disutility effort $2 → reservation $8; rent $4/h × 44 weeks × 35 h = 1540 h → $6,160), 6.6 (labour discipline: B=(10,0.7), A=(12,0.5); axes effort per hour (vertical) vs hourly wage (horizontal)). Best response curve starts at reservation wage, concave increasing, slope = MRT. Isocost for effort rays e=(const)w; steeper = cheaper effort; slope MRS. Equilibrium A tangency (Nash equilibrium); efficiency wage > reservation wage. Involuntary unemployment makes rent real. Rent per hour = (w − effort disutility) − (benefit − unemployment disutility); total = rent/h × expected hours unemployed. Variants: effort disutility $1 → res $7, rent $5×1540=$7,700; benefit $5 → same. Isocost through B: 0.07 effort per $; at w=12 → 0.84 (infeasible).
9 WS/PS (W10) TE Unit 9: §9.3 WS curve, §9.5 PS curve, nominal/real wage, labour productivity; §9.10 wage bargaining & union voice effects; Fig 9.12 (adapted). WS upward (higher employment → shorter unemployment spell → smaller rent → must pay more); never touches labour supply (full employment → zero rent). PS horizontal w=λ(1−µ) (λ average product of labour, µ markup). Equilibrium X; involuntary unemployment gap to labour supply. Shifts: benefits ↑, stronger unions → WS up → employment ↓ (real wage unchanged); limiting strikes → WS down; λ↑ or µ↓ → PS up → higher wage & employment. Union voice effect pushes WS down; net ambiguous. Immigration shifts labour supply right. Labour market rates: participation = LF/WAP; employment rate = E/WAP; unemployment rate = U/LF. Country A: WAP 4.5m LF 3.5m E 3.2m U 0.3m → 77.78%, 71.11%, 8.57%; Country B: WAP 30.7, LF 20.8, E 17.2, U 3.6 → 67.75%, 56.03%, 17.31%.
10 External effects (W11) TE Unit 12: §12.1 MPC/MSC/MEC (& MPB/MSB/MEB), §12.2 Coasean bargaining, §12.3 Pigouvian tax/subsidy; cap and trade (ref truncated "Section 20." — Fig 20.16, §20.5; Unit 21 §§21.1, 21.8); Fig 12.2 (bananas & Weevokil pesticide harming fishermen). MSC=MPC+MEC; private P=MPC, social P=MSC; DWL triangle between MSC and price from Qsoc to Qpriv. Four policies (quota, Pigouvian tax = MEC at Qsoc, Coasean bargaining, compensation) reach same Q but differ in distribution. Coase limits (transaction costs, few parties, enforceable rights, no wealth effects). Positive externalities → subsidy = MEB. Cap and trade: equal marginal abatement cost at X; trading from 50–50 allocation (C,D); shaded saving. Efficient pollution not zero. Fig 12.2 T/F: rights to fishermen → firm stops? false; producers highest profit if rights to them: true; MEC = pesticide cost? false.
11 Lorenz curve & Gini (W12) TE §5.12 (skip first two paragraphs), §5.13 Operation Barga; Unit 19 except §19.4; §19.7 labour market segmentation; §19.10 market/disposable/final income, welfare state, progressive/regressive; Fig 5.14a; also Figs 19.20 (WS/PS with Lorenz), 19.21 segmentation, 19.22 automation, 19.25 globalisation. Lorenz: cumulative population share (poorest first) x vs cumulative income share y; 45° equality. G=A/(A+B)=2A=1−2B; small group exact G = n/(n−1) × diagram value (max (n−1)/n). Mean-difference formula G=ΣΣ|xi−xj|/(2n(n−1)x̄) [for exact small-sample]. Examples: 80 farmers 20% land → G=0.6; incomes 2,5,20 → diagram 0.4444, exact 0.667 (ΣΣ|diff| = 72, /(2·3·2·9)=72/108). Fig 5.14a: 90 farmers no land, 10 landowners all → G=0.9. Market income → disposable (+cash transfers −direct taxes) → final (+benefits in kind −indirect taxes); Gini falls each step. Progressive vs regressive (share of income). Policy Q2: lower inheritance-tax threshold, raise capital gains tax, relax child-benefit eligibility.
